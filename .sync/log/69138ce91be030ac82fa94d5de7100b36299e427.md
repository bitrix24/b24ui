## The finding: a second Vue runtime, and whose it is

`@vue/runtime-core` goes from **one** resolved version to **two** — 3.5.42
beside 3.5.43. Two Vue runtimes is the classic split-reactivity, broken-`inject`,
failing-`instanceof` shape, so it needed attributing rather than noting.

`pnpm why` gives the chain:

```
@vue/runtime-core@3.5.42
└─ @vue/runtime-dom@3.5.42
   └─ @vue/server-renderer@3.5.42
      └─ @vue/test-utils@2.5.1
```

`@vue/test-utils@2.5.1` depends on `@vue/server-renderer@3.5.42` exactly. While
the app was on 3.5.42 the two agreed and there was one copy; moving `vue` to
3.5.43 split them.

**Not introduced here.** Upstream's own lockfile at this commit carries
`@vue/runtime-core` 3.5.42 *and* 3.5.43, and `@vue/server-renderer` likewise,
from the same `@vue/test-utils@2.5.1`. Inherited, not adapted in.

Scope is the test renderer, and it resolves itself when test-utils catches up.
Recorded rather than worked around: pinning `@vue/test-utils` back to 2.5.0
would diverge from upstream to avoid a duplication upstream also has.

**Method note.** A hand-rolled reverse-dependency walk over `pnpm-lock.yaml`
gave a wrong answer — it reported `@vue/shared@3.5.43` as the parent of
`@vue/server-renderer@3.5.42`, which is a sibling key, not a parent. The
lockfile was not re-parsed more carefully; `pnpm why` was asked instead. Same
lesson as the `node_modules/.pnpm` counting in the `9c99bf16` journal: ask the
package manager, do not re-implement it.
