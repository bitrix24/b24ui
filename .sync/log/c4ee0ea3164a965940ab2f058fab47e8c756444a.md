## The regression, read from the installed source rather than the changelog

Nuxt 4.5 stopped prefetching `custom` links itself. `nuxt-link.js` in
`nuxt@4.5.2` — the version this fork declares — guards **both** triggers:

- `:225` `if (import.meta.client && !props.custom)` — the visibility observer;
- `:297` `if (!props.custom) { … shouldPrefetch('interaction') … }` — the hover
  and focus handlers.

`Link.vue` here always renders `<NuxtLink … custom>`, so **neither fired**: no
prefetch on visibility, none on interaction. Nothing errors and nothing warns;
navigations are simply slower than they were.

The same file exposes the replacement API to the slot at `:284` — `prefetched`
and `shouldPrefetch` — which is what makes re-wiring possible rather than a
reimplementation, and `:210` computes `props.custom ? ref?.$el?.nextElementSibling
: ref?.$el`, which is where the "root is a fragment anchor, the element is its
next sibling" step below comes from.

## b24ui port

`src/runtime/utils/prefetch.ts` is taken as upstream wrote it: a
`requestIdleCallback` compat pair and one shared `IntersectionObserver`. Its
header note applies here unchanged — kept out of `utils/link.ts` so the Vue
builds, which share `LinkBase` and never render `NuxtLink`, do not load it.

`Link.vue` takes the wiring with the fork's standing renames only: `b24ui` for
`ui` in the class call, and `B24LinkBase` for `ULinkBase`. Our `resolveLinkClass`
keeps its extra `isAction` variant, so `prefetchedClass` joins the existing
`class` rather than replacing upstream's shorter call.

Both branches of the template are wired, as upstream does: the `custom` slot,
where the consumer renders the element, and the `B24LinkBase` branch, where we
do.

## Tests

`test/components/nuxt/LinkPrefetch.spec.ts`, ported as written — five cases
covering both triggers, the preservation of a caller's own `pointerenter` and
`focus` handlers, and the two opt-outs (`prefetchOn: 'interaction'` must not
observe visibility; `noPrefetch` must do neither).

They assert against the `link:prefetch` hook on the Nuxt app rather than against
markup, and drive the `IntersectionObserver` by hand, happy-dom's being inert.
That is what makes them able to see this port at all — worth saying next to
`6caa6a95`, ported four commits earlier, where the equivalent handler is never
invoked under happy-dom and its tests are green with or without the fix.
